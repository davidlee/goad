# A reviewer's supporting example is not evidence until someone else checks it

Learned at slice 004's plan review, where a false clause travelled from a
finding's body into the plan itself.

## The fact

The reviewer supplied a supporting example inside one finding, repeated it in
its round-3 reply, and it was adopted whole into the plan — the claim that the
design restated neither of two requirements, when it restated both. It was
caught only because a later reviewer had been told explicitly to check the
orchestrator's own edits hardest.

A finding's *claim* gets scrutinised. The example the finding offers in support
of it usually does not, by anyone — the raiser believes it, and the responder
is arguing about the claim.

## The rule

**A reviewer is the last person who will check its own example.** An example
adopted from a finding is verified by whoever writes it into a document, not by
the finding's author.

## Why it matters more than it looks

A wrong reason beside a right one is worse than no reason. A reader who checks
the wrong one has cause to doubt the right one — and to doubt the document that
carries both.
